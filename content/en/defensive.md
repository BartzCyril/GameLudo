Being sent back to your base is the worst thing that can happen in Ludo. A good defence means **blocking your opponents' pawns**, **using your advanced pawns as shields** and above all **avoiding the squares where you can be captured**.

## Know the danger zone

An opponent's pawn can reach any square **1 to 6 squares in front of it**: that is its danger zone. If one of your pawns is in it, it can be captured on the next roll.

The risk grows with the number of opponents: a pawn in the danger zone of two opponents' pawns is twice as exposed.

So before every move, spot:

- **the opponents' pawns behind your pawns**;
- **the squares after an opponent's starting square**, where new pawns can appear as soon as they roll a 6.

## Block your opponents' pawns

Placing your pawns so that they **slow down your opponents** makes them lose time:

- **threaten an opponent's pawn** by placing yourself just behind it: its owner may have to use it to escape rather than to move their other pawns;
- **occupy the track before their home column**: an opponent who fears being captured will hesitate to get closer;
- in the home column, remember that **you can't move past a pawn already there**: placing your own pawns well keeps you from blocking yourself ([see the end-of-game rules](@end)).

## Use your advanced pawns as shields

Your most advanced pawns can protect the others:

- an advanced pawn placed **between an opponent and your weaker pawns** acts as an obstacle: the opponent has to deal with it before reaching the others;
- an advanced pawn also **threatens the opponents** who overtake it, which makes them more careful;
- and an advanced pawn is **close to home**: as soon as it enters the home column, it is safe for good.

Careful: a shield is still an exposed pawn. Don't sacrifice a pawn that is nearly home to protect one that has just come out.

## Choose the safest move

When no attacking move stands out, choose the move that **reduces the risk the most**:

1. **Move the most threatened pawn away**, especially if it is far ahead.
2. **Stay behind your opponents** rather than just in front of them.
3. **Get into the home column** as soon as the exact roll allows it: it is the only permanent protection.
4. **Avoid bunching your pawns** in front of the same opponent: a single lucky roll could cost you a lot.

## Defend without becoming passive

Too cautious a defence makes you fall behind. The best approach is to alternate: **attack when the opportunity is good**, as explained in the article on [attacking strategies](@offensive), and **protect your pawns** the rest of the time. The [observation and adaptation strategies](@observation) help you know when to switch from one to the other.

## Key takeaways

- The six squares in front of an opponent's pawn are dangerous.
- Stay behind your opponents when possible.
- Your advanced pawns protect the others, but don't sacrifice them.
- A pawn in the home column is safe for good.
