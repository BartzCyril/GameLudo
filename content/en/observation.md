You can't control the die, but **you can control how you use it**. The observation and adaptation strategy is about **watching the other players' moves**, **spotting their weaknesses** and **adjusting your plan** every turn, while protecting your own pawns.

## Read the board every turn

Before rolling the die, take a few seconds to read the situation:

- **threats**: which opponents' pawns are 1 to 6 squares behind yours?
- **opportunities**: which opponents' pawns can you reach with a single roll?
- **the race**: which player has the most pawns home, or closest to home?
- **reserves**: how many pawns does each player still have in their base? A player with no pawn on the track can't threaten you until they roll a 6.

Once the die is rolled, compare the possible moves in light of these observations, instead of automatically moving your most advanced pawn.

## Spot your opponents' weaknesses

Every player has weak spots. Spotting them gives you an edge:

- **an opponent moving only one pawn** is vulnerable: capturing it sends them almost back to square one;
- **a pawn stuck at the entrance of its home column** is waiting for the exact roll: it stays still and can become a target;
- **a player who keeps their pawns bunched** in front of yours offers you several possible captures;
- **a very cautious player** moves slowly: you can get ahead in the race.

Exploit these weaknesses while keeping an eye on your own pawns.

## Adapt your play to the stage of the game

The right strategy changes as the game goes on.

**At the start of the game**, bring your pawns out and take up space on the track. Captures are frequent but cost little, because the pawns have not gone far yet. The [rules for starting a game](@start) explain this phase.

**In the middle of the game**, the balance between [attack](@offensive) and [defence](@defensive) is key. It is the time to move several pawns and target the most advanced opponents.

**At the end of the game**, every capture counts enormously. Get your pawns into the home column and respect the right order of arrival, explained in the [end-of-game rules](@end).

## Adapt your play to the score

- **You are ahead**: play it safe. Keep your pawns out of the danger zones and get them home as soon as possible.
- **It is close**: stay balanced, taking the captures that don't expose you too much.
- **You are behind**: take more risks. Go after the player closest to winning first, even if your pawn is exposed afterwards.

## Playing against bots

On Gameludo, your opponents are bots. Watch how they play over several games: which pawns do they move first? Do they capture whenever they can? Spotting their habits will let you anticipate their moves and adapt your strategy.

## Key takeaways

- Read the board before every roll: threats, opportunities, the race and reserves.
- Spot your opponents' weaknesses and exploit them.
- Play safe when ahead, take risks when behind.
